Final-year research · Cattle farming

One herd record for disease, feed, birth, and the milk cheque.

Smart Farm is the full research system for a Sri Lankan cattle farmer. A photograph can name a disease or a behaviour. Weight is typed in from a scale or tape. The same app then plans feed and nutrition, estimates a calving window or a hatch, forecasts next month’s milk income in rupees, and keeps every result on that animal’s record.

The whole project

Four components, one phone path.

The research covers the week a small herd actually has: health, feeding, reproduction, and the market. Flutter is the farmer’s screen. Flask serves every model. Laravel keeps the animal, the user, and the history.

Illangasinhe I.M.A.D.

Disease detection and emergency alerts

Cameras and sensors look for abnormal behaviour or symptoms. DenseNet121 and YOLOv8x keep the stronger disease call. The system recommends a first action or notifies a veterinarian.

Jayawardhana H.A.S.

Automated feeding and nutrition

Cameras read type and size. Body weight is a manual weight measurement. Feed type, amount, and schedule follow that weight, breed, and health, and the system can dispense the ration.

Shamika G.L.

Birth and egg hatch prediction

Image, sensor, and historical records estimate whether a calf will be born healthy or an egg will hatch.

Arachchi W.A.A.I.W.

Live market prediction

A live selling and buying record, with an AI forecast of price and a recommendation on when to buy or sell livestock, milk, eggs, or meat.

What the farmer gets

Six decisions, one herd record.

Each function sits behind a screen the farmer already understands: a photograph when the sign is visible, a measured weight when feed is planned, and a clear result either way.

01

Disease, severity, treatment

Two vision models score the same image. The system keeps the more confident result, then grades the case mild, moderate, or severe and suggests a treatment.

02

Behaviour as an early warning

YOLOv8s reads eating, standing, lying, walking, and related behaviours. A video timeline shows when a pattern drifts from that animal’s baseline.

03

Manual weight and a daily ration

The farmer enters a manual weight measurement from a scale or tape. Feed then follows that weight, breed, age, milk yield, and activity.

04

Nutrition matched to the animal

Eleven health and production inputs set dry-matter intake and minerals, so a sick or high-yielding cow is not fed like the herd average.

05

Birth window and hatch chance

A neural model estimates whether calving is close. A hybrid model scores an egg from temperature, humidity, weight, turning, and days incubated.

06

Next month’s milk income

Price, litres, fat, solids-not-fat, disease stage, feed quality, and lactation month produce a rupee forecast for price and income.

Why this research

Late news is expensive on a small farm.

Mastitis, foot-and-mouth disease, and lumpy skin are often noticed only after milk drops or a lesion is obvious. Feed is guessed. Calving is a surprise. The milk cheque is explained after the month has closed.

Published tools usually solve one of those problems. This project joins disease detection, behaviour, a manually measured weight, feed and nutrition, birth and hatch, and a rupee forecast in a single phone workflow for a Sri Lankan herd.

1

See

The farmer photographs the animal for disease, behaviour, or identity, and enters a manual weight measurement when a ration is needed. Cattle identity stays with the herd record.

2

Judge

Flask services run the vision and tabular models. Disease detection compares DenseNet121 and YOLOv8x and returns the stronger call.

3

Act

The app shows severity, a treatment suggestion, a feed or nutrition plan, a birth or hatch estimate, or a rupee forecast.

4

Remember

Detections, feeds, births, hatches, and market checks stay with the cow profile so the next decision has a history.
