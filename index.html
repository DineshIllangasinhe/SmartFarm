Final-year research · Cattle farming

From a traditional herd to a farm that can see, decide, and warn.

This project builds one mobile system for cattle farmers. A photograph can name a disease, clinical values can grade its severity, and the same app can plan feed, anticipate a birth, and forecast next month’s milk income in Sri Lankan rupees.

What the farmer gets

Six decisions, one herd record.

Each function is a trained model behind a screen the farmer already understands: a photo, a few numbers, and a clear result.

Health

Disease, severity, treatment

Two vision models score the same image. The system keeps the more confident result, then a gradient-boosting model grades mild, moderate, or severe and suggests a treatment.

Behaviour

Early warning from how the cow moves

YOLOv8s reads eating, standing, lying, walking, and related behaviours. A video timeline shows when a pattern drifts from the animal’s own baseline.

Feeding

Weight and a daily ration

A segmentation and regression network estimates weight from a photo. Feed amount then follows breed, age, milk yield, and activity.

Nutrition

A ration matched to the animal

Eleven health and production inputs drive a recommender for dry-matter intake and minerals, so a sick or high-yielding cow is not fed like the herd average.

Reproduction

Birth window and hatch chance

A neural model estimates whether calving is close. A hybrid network and random forest score an egg from temperature, humidity, weight, turning, and days incubated.

Market

Next month’s milk income

Price, litres, fat, solids-not-fat, disease stage, feed quality, and lactation month produce a rupee forecast for price change, price per litre, and income.

Why this research

Late news is expensive on a small farm.

Mastitis, foot-and-mouth disease, and lumpy skin are often noticed only after milk drops or a lesion is obvious. Feed is guessed. Calving is a surprise. The milk cheque is explained after the month has closed.

Published tools usually solve one of those problems. This project joins vision, clinical values, behaviour, and a market model in a single phone workflow for a Sri Lankan herd.

1

See

The farmer photographs the animal or uploads a short video. Cattle identity can be stored with the herd record.

2

Judge

Flask services run the vision and tabular models. Disease detection compares DenseNet121 and YOLOv8x and returns the stronger call.

3

Act

The app shows severity, a treatment suggestion, a feed or nutrition plan, a birth or hatch estimate, or a rupee forecast.

4

Remember

Detections, feeds, births, hatches, and market checks stay with the cow profile so the next decision has a history.
